# Contributing to WREH

WREH is a research programme and research community. Contributions are welcome when they preserve mathematical traceability, claim discipline, and the distinction between an operational result and an ontological interpretation.

## Contribution classes

Every substantive contribution should identify its status explicitly as one or more of:

- **THEOREM** — a formal result with stated hypotheses and proof;
- **MODEL** — a declared mathematical or physical model, including its assumptions;
- **NUMERICAL EVIDENCE** — reproducible computation or simulation;
- **PHYSICAL HYPOTHESIS** — a proposed physical interpretation not established by the formalism alone;
- **INTERPRETATION** — conceptual or philosophical reading of established results;
- **OPEN QUESTION** — an unresolved problem or obligation.

Do not silently promote a MODEL, numerical pattern, or interpretation into a theorem or physical fact.

## Claim discipline

Before proposing a new physical interpretation, check [registry/CLAIM_FIREWALLS.md](registry/CLAIM_FIREWALLS.md).

In particular:

- response equivalence does not imply ontological identity;
- protocol/refinement order is not physical time or dynamics without an independent construction;
- mathematical admissibility does not imply physical existence;
- Boundary Compensation objects are not automatically WREH cosmological or spacetime objects.

## New-paper discipline

The WREH sequence is obligation-driven. A new numbered paper is justified only by a new mathematical or physical obligation that is not already owned by an existing work.

Before opening a new technical paper, state:

1. the new mathematical target;
2. the upstream objects it inherits;
3. the precise novelty boundary relative to existing WREH work and prior art;
4. the claim ceiling;
5. a failure criterion or acceptable negative result;
6. the intended downstream handoff.

## Corrections and negative results

Counterexamples, no-go results, failed conjectures, prior-art reductions, and corrections are first-class scientific contributions. They should not be hidden merely because they narrow the programme.

## Pull requests

For substantial changes:

1. use a focused branch;
2. keep scientific changes separate from formatting-only changes when practical;
3. explain which claims are added, removed, strengthened, or weakened;
4. include citations for nontrivial prior-art claims;
5. preserve source files and reproducibility material needed to audit the result.

## Citation and lineage

When a WREH result depends materially on a prior WREH or Boundary Compensation result, cite the exact work and DOI when available. Cross-programme transfer must state the bridge explicitly.

## Licence

No repository-wide content or software licence has yet been assigned. Until an explicit licence is added, do not infer reuse rights from public repository visibility.
