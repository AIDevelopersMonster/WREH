# WR-II prior-art and novelty audit — v0.1

**Document:** WR-II — Finite Consistency and Global World Realizability  
**Date:** 2026-10-07  
**Audit stage:** mathematical-spine preflight

## Executive finding

The local-to-global problem is highly non-new in its abstract mathematical
ingredients. WR-II is viable only with a narrow architectural claim.

The strongest defensible WREH distinction is the following package:

1. start from the WR-I class of admissible global realizations;
2. use finite protocol-response images as the local data;
3. construct the finite-consistency inverse limit;
4. embed the full WR-I response quotient into that completion;
5. define the residual complement as a model-relative global-realizability defect;
6. distinguish full finite consistency from restricted-context consistency.

No priority claim should be made for compactness, inverse limits,
finite-intersection principles, extension theorems, or global-section
obstructions.

## Closest prior lines

### Compactness and inverse limits

Classical topology and inverse-limit theory already supply many finite-to-global
existence theorems. The WR-II compactness results are applications of these
principles to the WR-I response architecture.

Novelty impact: strong narrowing.

### Kolmogorov extension

Compatible finite-dimensional probability distributions can, under standard
hypotheses, define a global probability law or stochastic process.

Difference: WR-II response profiles are not assumed to be probability measures,
and the carrier is an admissible world class rather than a family of marginals.

Novelty impact: domain-specific positive archetype; no conflict if stated
explicitly.

### Vorob'ev (1962)

N. N. Vorob'ev, "Consistent Families of Measures and Their Extensions",
Theory of Probability and Its Applications 7(2), 147–163.
DOI: 10.1137/1107014.

Vorob'ev gives a structural criterion for when consistent local families of
measures extend globally.

Novelty impact: direct predecessor for the context-family layer.

### Abramsky–Brandenburger (2011)

S. Abramsky and A. Brandenburger,
"The Sheaf-Theoretic Structure of Non-Locality and Contextuality",
New Journal of Physics 13, 113036.
DOI: 10.1088/1367-2630/13/11/113036.

Contextuality is formulated as obstruction to global sections over a
measurement cover.

Novelty impact: very close local-to-global predecessor. WR-II must not claim
that compatible local data can fail to have a global realization is new.

### Atserias–Kolaitis (2023)

A. Atserias and P. G. Kolaitis,
"Consistency, Acyclicity, and Positive Semirings",
Springer, 2023, pp. 623–668.
DOI: 10.1007/978-3-031-24117-8_17.

This work unifies local/global consistency phenomena from probability and
database theory and characterizes global consistency via acyclicity in a broad
positive-semiring setting.

Novelty impact: strong evidence that local/global consistency is mature
cross-domain theory.

## Important architecture distinction discovered

If the full finite power set Fin(P) is used and P itself is finite, then the
full bundle belongs to the index family. Therefore finite consistency already
includes global realizability and the WR-II defect is empty.

Thus a nontrivial WR-II defect requires at least one of:

- an infinite protocol family; or
- a restricted context family that omits the full joint protocol bundle.

This distinction must remain explicit throughout the paper.

## Current claim ceiling

WR-II may claim:

- the WREH-specific construction of the finite-response completion attached to
  a WR-I world-response structure;
- its canonical comparison with the WR-I full response quotient;
- the model-relative defect object;
- explicit toy counterexamples;
- compactness, closed-image, and shrinking-fibre criteria as proved
  applications.

WR-II must not claim:

- invention of finite-to-global consistency;
- invention of inverse-limit completion;
- invention of contextual/global-section obstruction;
- invention of compactness-based extension;
- physical existence or nonexistence from mathematical completion alone.
